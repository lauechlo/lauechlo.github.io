Hong Kong, a harp, and how I ended up in product.

People ask how a psychology major ended up running product. The answer starts four years before I declared the major.

A few things about me

I grew up between classical music and code. The plan was a psychology degree, and product is just what it turned into.

Psychology taught me to ask questions without leading the answer, and the music cognition lab got me comfortable sitting with 300 free-text responses until one signal shows up. Hoagie is where I figured out that the gap between “I don't know how to build this” and “I built this” is mostly just starting.

Ten weeks on Disney+ Search in Seattle was the same thing at a much bigger scale, including a spec of mine that died at intake in week three. Mostly I got better at deciding while I was still scared of being wrong.

Outside of work I play the harp, sing jazz a cappella, hike whenever I can find a trail, and drink an unreasonable amount of cheese foam boba.

What I actually want next is to be the one catching up, around people who are better than me at the things I care about. Somewhere growing fast enough that I get handed things before I am ready for them.

I'm President of the Princeton Katzenjammers, the oldest co-ed a cappella group in the Ivy League. This is what we sound like, recorded for our 50th anniversary.
